Middlemarch

George Eliot

4.2
A study of provincial life.

Middlemarch follows the lives of the people of a small English town in the years before the 1832 Reform Act: Dorothea Brooke, who marries an elderly scholar, the young doctor Tertius Lydgate, who wants to reform medicine, and the families around them, whose ambitions and mistakes slowly change the town. George Eliot published the novel in eight parts in 1871 and 1872.

George Eliot was the pen name of Mary Ann Evans (1819-1880), one of the leading English novelists of her time.

About this edition

This edition follows the text of the second edition of 1874 and adds a chronology of the author's life, notes on the historical background of the Reform Act, a map of the town and its surroundings, and a short guide for reading groups. The introduction explains how the novel grew out of two separate stories, one about a young doctor and one about a young woman who wants to do something useful with her life, and how the two were joined into one book.

Reading guide: Dune

Before you start

Dune is a long novel with its own vocabulary. Do not stop at every unfamiliar word: most of them become clear from the context within a few pages, and the glossary at the end of the book is there when they do not.

Our staff suggest reading the first hundred pages in one or two sittings. The book introduces many characters and houses early on, and the story moves much faster once they are in place.

Book one

The first part follows a family moving to a new and hostile planet. Pay attention to who trusts whom: most of the later events grow out of the loyalties and betrayals set up here.

Short quotations from in-world books open every chapter. They are easy to skip, but they often tell you what the chapter is about before it begins.

Book two

The second part slows down and spends more time in the desert. This is where the book's ideas about ecology, water and survival come forward, and where many readers decide whether they love the novel.

If you are reading with a group, this is a good place for a first meeting: everyone has met the main characters, and nobody has reached the ending yet.

Book three

The last part is the shortest and the fastest. Events that took a hundred pages in the first part now take a few, so read it when you have time to finish it.

Questions for a reading group

Which character changed your opinion of them the most, and at which point? How does the desert shape the people who live in it? Would the story work in a different setting?

The appendices at the end of the book are optional. Many readers come back to them after a second reading, when the history behind the story becomes more interesting than the plot.
